As water bears through natural blowholes, it slowly the layers of sandstone. The end result is an area of shoreline populated by rock formations that look unlike uneven of pancakes. Thanks to a number of walkways in the area you can really get a close look at the structures without risk of damaging them.

Located in Maranhão state, Lençóis Maranhenses National Park extends from the coastline to cover a total of 380,000 acres of land. The inland area is made up of low-lying sand dunes that, following rainstorms, become interspersed with fresh water lagoons. The end result is truly and to the point that, whether photographed from above or seen in person, the site is hard to believe. Goblin Valley State Park, USA

▶ Open video

Not as cute but nonetheless an interesting place to visit, Goblin Valley State Park is thankfully not named after a dominant local species. Located in Utah a few hundred miles from Salt Lake City, it actually takes its name from the strange rock formations that populate this otherwise Valley. Slowly but surely, the powerful elements have at the sandstone, resulting in mushroom-shaped pinnacles and hoodoos. These unique natural structures tend to be top-heavy with thinner lower sections, due to the upper layers of being more durable. Many of the pinnacles and hoodoos are millions of years old, and their odd shape certainly feels prehistoric — some suggest they even look like monstrous creatures from fairy tales, hence the name.

Catacombs, Paris, France

Most of the weird places on our list today are occurring, but not all of them. The world-famous Paris Catacombs might be a popular attraction, but there's no denying that this underground shrine to death is just about one of the strangest things the city has to offer. Occupying a small section of the tunnels that run underneath this city of love, the catacombs contain the bones of 6 million Parisians. Once a simple dumping ground for human due to overloaded cemeteries, the ossuary was better organized in the early 19th century, when the bones were set up in various patterns. You're sure to enjoy the wine, - food and stunning architecture of Paris, but we can all but guarantee that the catacombs are what will really stay with you.

A powerful saltwater fountain, the aptly named coastal feature is known for both its seemingly insatiable thirst and the watery explosions it emits at high tide. Moeraki Boulders, NZ

Rocks — they're not always the most exciting things on the planet, but as soon as they take on a weird shape, texture or color, or in an location, we can't but be fascinated by them. And the Moeraki Boulders check more than one box. Noteworthy for their size, location and strangely smooth shape, they almost look like the fossilized eggs of some colossal primordial beast, as if they were left to hatch by a sea turtle the size of a football stadium. the beaches of New Zealand's Otago Coast, they vary in size from 1.8 feet to a 7.2 feet in diameter. Though they've inspired local myths and extraterrestrial conspiracy theories, scientists for their part deem them to be naturally occurring, albeit having taken shape under specific circumstances across millions of years.

Imagine yourself swimming in a lake completely surrounded by jellyfish. For some, this particular mental image might be the stuff of nightmares — but hey, weird places aren't for everyone. And for travelers looking for a thoroughly unique experience, it doesn't get much better or more surreal than this. Located in Palau on the island of Eil Malk, this marine lake is to the with jellyfish. The lake is only about 1,500 feet long and 520 feet deep at its widest points, but in this modest-sized lake swim millions of jellyfish that are largely from the ocean and have diverged from the evolutionary path of the rest of their various species. the , these jellyfish can sting, but they're not considered powerful enough to harm humans. Lake Hillier & Hutt Lagoon, Australia

▶ Open video

Lakes in many and — but color? That's not usually a variable you expect. In Australia, however, there are actually multiple lakes that defy convention with the bright pink color of their water. Lake Hillier, located on Middle Island, was first documented by European explorer Matthew Flinders in 1802. Today we know that the unique pink color is a result of two factors: the incredibly high concentration of salt in the lake and the presence of Dunaliella salina. Despite the - pink hue, Lake Hillier is safe to swim in, though notably hard to reach. For a pink lake that's more accessible, consider a trip to Hutt Lagoon on Australia's West Coast — weather conditions and time of year can affect the intensity of the color.

Deadvlei, Namibia

▶ Open video

If you're looking for a desert oasis, you're too late by about 600 to 700 years, actually. If, however, you're in the market for a totally unique photo, Deadvlei is just about as of a as you can ask for. This salt pan in Namibia is surrounded on all sides by some of the highest sand dunes on Earth. At one point long ago this was a collection point for water after rainfall, giving rise to an oasis of camel-thorn trees. It eventually dried up, however, and now only the skeletal remains are left as a reminder of what once was. The heat and lack of humidity are so intense that the trees, while not petrified, have become blackened as a result of the intense elements — and it's beautiful.

The world's largest salt pan, this 4,086-square-mile stretch of land is covered in a salt crust and is one of the expanses on Earth. It makes for a remarkable sight on any given day, rain or shine, but when a thin layer of water collects on the surface it becomes one giant mirror that reflects the sky — and when that happens, your brain will genuinely struggle to process the sight before your eyes: sky and land into one as far as the eye can see. For the avid traveller who prefers to seek out unique experiences, the scenic coast of Nova Scotia offers more than just lighthouses. On the outskirts of the main tourist hubs, you can find some truly bizarre and eye-catching attractions. One such place is the "Balancing Rock," a whopping 9-meter tall basalt column that appears to defy gravity. The hike to see this geological marvel is awe-inspiringly beautiful, culminating in a breathtaking view of the uninhabited islands scattered across the bay.

Further inland, if you venture into the less-trodden paths, you might discover the charming, isolated village of Blue Rocks. Unlike the capital, which can be packed to the gills with tourists in summer, this village offers a sense of peace. Its vibrant blue-hued fishing shacks are an attraction in their own right, providing a surreal and photogenic landscape. For many, these moments of discovery become the highlights of the trip, offering a full immersion into the local culture and untouched nature.
