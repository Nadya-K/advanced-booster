The average millionaire is 57 years old. I did it by age 27. And while there's not get rich quick scheme - there are 5 MAJOR secrets to becoming rich, and I promise this video is not clickbait. Lots of people chalk success up to things like luck or being born into the right family, and while those things can make it a lot easier - understanding yourself & how the game of money is played is just as important. You see - our generation, think millennials and Gen-Z have lived most of our lives in financial turmoil. We experienced things like 9/11, the 2008 housing market crash, and even a global pandemic. Not to mention housing prices and education costs have skyrocketed and inflation is off the charts, while wages have stagnated. So it's no surprise that becoming a millionaire can feel more and more out of reach if you weren't born to rich parents. But - the stats actually tell a different story. A 2019 study published by Wealth-X found that around 68% of those with a net worth of $30 million or more made it themselves. Furthermore, a second study by Fidelity Investments found that 88% of all millionaires are self-made, meaning they did not inherit their wealth. And listen - coming into wealth isn't always a simple process – many people work very hard to achieve their financial success, But I'm Vivian, Your Rich BFF, and your favorite Wall Street girly, and I'm going to teach you major secrets that helped me become a millionaire in half the time it takes most people. Please like, comment, and subscribe to learn more about being good with your money! Let's get to it!

Lesson #1

You can only save as much as you earn: First and foremost, rich people are not scrimping and saving. And I know what you're thinking - duh they're rich. But that's not what I mean. We as a society often point the finger when it comes to financial shortcomings. We blame individuals because they're overspenders, they're shopaholics, and they loveeee lattes and avocado toast. But rich people know a big secret - you can only save as much as you earn. This essentially means, it's a lot easier to come up with extra dollars by MAKING MORE than it is by cutting back on every little purchase that brings you joy. Instead of worrying about a $5 coffee, millionaires are a lot more focused on how they can level up their careers, so that at the end of each and every year - they can ask for a raise or a promotion. When most people think about self-made millionaires, they imagine some entrepreneur working around the clock, but 39% of the self-made millionaires actually get rich working for someone else, so performing well at your job - is an important part of getting to millionaire status.

Lesson #2

Labor vs. Growth: Millionaires think a lot about scalability. Most of us trade time and labor for money, but millionaires know that these are both finite resources. We only have 24 hours in a day, 7 days a week, 365 days in a year. So if your money making is dependent on your time and labor - you're not going to have much time or energy for anything else. What's the point of being rich if you can't enjoy a nice vacation, make it in time for your kid's soccer game, or have a nice meal out with friends and family? Instead, millionaires all invest. They do so because they know while they can only work so many hours in a day - their money can work for them non-stop. By investing, they're able to bring in passive income aka where they work once, and reap the benefits over and over again. While they do focus on making as much as possible from their jobs, they then use this money to make them even more.

Lesson #3

Discipline & delaying gratification: Millionaires practice discipline and delayed gratification. I will be the one to say what everyone is wondering. No - millionaires are not smarter, better, faster, stronger, or anything special vs. Non-millionaires. The only marked difference I've noticed amongst my friends who are self-made millionaires vs. those who aren't is that they're typically more disciplined, and better at delaying gratification. For example - think about the runner's trick. You aren't running for 30 minutes, you're just running for 10 minutes 3 times. Runners think about running to the next stop sign, and then the next tree, then the landmark, and you're essentially able to mentally trick yourself into delaying gratification aka stopping. This is the same when it comes to wealth, because if you're able to delay comforts like luxury goods, or paying for convenience earlier on in your life, you'll be able to put your money to work early, meaning you don't have to put as much money away overall. This allows you to invest early and often, so that by the time you're 30 or 40 - you'll have a major financial cushion and can then start to really enjoy the fruits of your labor. If present you is able to take care of future you, instead of only focusing on present you, your life will get easier and easier. And I know we've all heard of the phrase - live every day like it's your last, but realistically - it's not. If you focus on making each day as spectacular as possible, especially by using your money to do so, there may not be much left over for you a week, a month, a year, or 10 years from now. I'm not saying don't have fun, but balancing fun today with a mix of responsibility and discipline, means you'll get to have fun in the future too.

Lesson #4

They love changing their minds: Many of us have heard the cliche term that millionaires aren't afraid of failure, but that's not the important part - the pivotal key here is that once these rich people recognize that they've failed - they're willing and able to change course quickly. When presented with new information, millionaires aren't shy about acknowledging that they're wrong so they waste less time trying to bail out a sinking ship. Whether it's business ideas, personal opinions, or even just directions when driving, millionaires are able to change their minds quickly and without shame. You doing so as well, will help you save time and money.

Lesson #5

They set ambitious goals: you know I always say this - but you don't get to become a millionaire if you don't make a plan. Very few millionaires just wing it and make it. Millionaires set ambitious goals and act on them. Not only do they write down their goals - they also formulate plans with baby steps to get there. It may not be feasible to scale your business from $0 to $1M overnight, but 25% growth each year over 4 years could get you there. It's unlikely for you to go from making $50k annually to $300k annually in one year, but if you have a plan to job jump every 2 years and ask for a 50% raise each time, you've certainly carved a roadmap on how you intend to do it. By making concrete plans that align with your goals - you'll set yourself up for success.

To close out this video - the most important thing to remember is that millionaires are not special. They're not smarter or better than non-millionaires, but they do live their lives more strategically, and if you're able to do the same - you could very well become a millionaire too. The more you're able to maximize these strategies - the faster it'll happen. If this video was helpful - subscribe for more money, wealth-building, and finance tips!

Linking devices

Linking devices are words and phrases, such as "therefore," "however," and "for example," that connect ideas, sentences, and paragraphs to create a logical flow and smooth transitions. They show relationships between different parts of a text, like cause and effect, comparison, contrast, and sequence, helping the reader follow the writer's train of thought.

Common types and examples

  • To add information: and, furthermore, moreover, additionally, also, in addition
  • To express reason or cause: because, since, as a result of, due to, for this reason
  • To express result or effect: therefore, so, as a result, consequently, thus, hence
  • To contrast ideas: however, but, on the other hand, by contrast, nevertheless, whereas, although
  • To compare ideas: similarly, likewise, in the same way, compared with
  • To provide examples: for example, for instance, such as, to illustrate
  • To show sequence: first, second(ly), next, then, finally, lastly
  • To summarize or conclude: in conclusion, to summarize, therefore, in summary, overall

Tips for using linking devices

  • Show relationships: Use linking devices to make the connection between ideas clear to the reader, such as explaining the cause of an event or the result of an action.
  • Ensure smooth flow: They prevent abrupt shifts between thoughts and ensure the text reads smoothly and logically.
  • Be mindful of placement: Linking words and phrases can often be placed at the beginning or in the middle of a sentence. Pay attention to punctuation, especially when a linking word starts a sentence (e.g., "However,…").
  • Vary your choices: Use different linking devices to avoid repetition and add variety to your writing.

5 Revision

5.1 Read the article

📖 Beyond the Grind: The Modern Blueprint for Building Wealth (click to collapse)

In today's economic landscape, it's common to chalk success up to sheer hustle. Yet, many find themselves navigating persistent financial turmoil as living costs skyrocket and home ownership prices seem to be off the charts. While corporate profits soar, for the average professional, real wages have stagnated, making financial stability feel more and more out of reach. Building a substantial net worth can appear as a puzzle with missing pieces.

So, what's the solution? First and foremost, we must move beyond the traditional advice simply to scrimp and save. It's easy to point the finger at personal financial shortcomings—the daily latte, the subscription services—and while it's wise to cut back on non-essentials, this alone is a defensive tactic. The modern wealth blueprint requires a powerful offensive strategy: to level up your career.

Gone are the days of expecting a generous raise or a promotion simply because you work around the clock. Loyalty often doesn't allow you to reap the benefits of your increased value. There is a marked difference between those who wait and those who strategically act.

This is where a refined concept of delaying gratification comes in. It's not just about skipping pleasures; it's about learning to mentally trick yourself into delaying gratification by linking today's choices to tomorrow's security. Every invested dollar or learned skill builds a financial cushion, so you can eventually enjoy the fruits of your labor without constant anxiety.

For many, the pivotal key has been career agility — the willingness and ability to change course quickly. This isn't about winging it. It means to set ambitious goals and execute a plan. The strategic job jump has become a central tool, but it's most effective when you first carve a roadmap identifying the skills, network, and roles that align with your targets.

The process culminates when you close out a chapter — a stagnant role, an underpaying job — and leap into one that recognizes your worth. This proactive approach transforms your financial trajectory, turning stagnant earnings into growing assets and shifting you from a mindset of scarcity to one of empowered building.

Ultimately, modern wealth isn't just about what you save, but what you earn through strategic career moves. It's a dynamic combination of disciplined saving, smart gratification delays, and, most crucially, the courage to continually invest in and advance your own professional value.
