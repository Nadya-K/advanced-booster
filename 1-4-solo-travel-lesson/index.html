I remember being in a rush, I was so incredibly hungry and I didn't have any snacks with me. And I stopped at one of those kiosks to get something to eat. I had just left security. And when I was at the kios paying for my sandwich or snack or whatever it was that I got, I must have put my laptop down. And then once I paid, I just walked away.

Must have done is a modal verb structure used to express a strong deduction or conclusion about a past event based on current evidence. It is used when you are very certain that something happened, but you are not stating it as a proven fact, but as a logical inference.

How to use "must have done"
Structure: Modal verb must + auxiliary verb have + past participle of the main verb.
Meaning: Expresses a high degree of certainty about a past action that is likely true.
Example: "The thief must have had a key, because the door was locked and nothing was broken".

Common contrasts and uses
Should have done: expresses regret or criticism for something that did not happen but was advisable.
Example: You should have called me; I was worried (implies you didn't call, but it would have been a good idea).
Can't have done: used to make a strong deduction that something did not happen.
Example: He can't have been at the party; I didn't see him there at all.
Might have done: used to express a lower degree of probability or a more tentative guess about a past event.
Example: It's raining, so they might have missed their train.

6 Speaking

Thursday question

Recall three solo travel mistakes from the video and talk about each of them.

Friday question — pick one you like the most

The phrase "trust your gut" is crucial for solo travellers. Can you think of a time (in travel or in life) when your intuition warned you about a person or a situation? How did you react? You can also talk about someone you know.

OR:
Which piece of advice do you think is the most universally important for any traveler, not just those traveling alone? Why?

Study buddy

1. If you had to summarize the author's philosophy on solo travel in one or two sentences, what would it be?

2. A common piece of advice is to avoid looking like you are in a rush, as it can make you a target. How can a traveller project confidence and awareness, even when they might be feeling lost or anxious?

3. Imagine a situation where a traveler is pressured into buying something they don't want. Is it ever better to be overly polite to avoid conflict, or is it sometimes necessary to make a scene to protect yourself? Where do you draw the line?

4. Solo travel is often associated with free spirits. Do you think this is a personality you are born with, or one that can be developed by stepping out of that box?

5. In the age of smartphones, is the ability to physically navigate with a paper map a lost art? What are the risks of being entirely dependent on digital maps while travelling?

6. To save on accommodation, many travellers take that red-eye flight. What are the pros and cons of this strategy? Would you do it?
